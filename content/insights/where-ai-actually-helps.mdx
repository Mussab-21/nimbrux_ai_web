## The AI Advice Problem

Every article about AI in business tells you the same thing: move fast, adopt everything, or get left behind. 

This is bad advice. It's also making a lot of organizations waste money on tools that don't move the needle on anything that matters.

Here's a more useful frame: before spending anything on AI, answer these 4 questions.

---

## The 4 Questions

### 1. What is the actual output I need?

Not "we want to use AI." What is the specific thing that should happen differently once the AI is in place?

- "Customer service emails are answered in under 2 hours" — concrete
- "We want to be more AI-enabled" — useless

If you can't describe the output, you're not ready to implement anything yet. That's fine — start with discovery, not deployment.

### 2. Where does most of the manual time go right now?

AI is best at repetitive tasks with pattern-based structure. The places worth automating:
- Document review and data extraction
- Routing and classification (support tickets, applications, leads)
- Content generation from structured inputs
- Report compilation from known data sources

The places *not* worth automating yet:
- Complex judgment calls that require context
- Tasks that happen fewer than 50 times per month
- Anything where errors are very expensive

### 3. What does the data situation look like?

AI systems need clean, structured data. If you don't have that, the first investment shouldn't be AI — it should be data organization.

An honest audit:
- Where does your operational data live? (Spreadsheets, CRMs, databases)
- How clean and consistent is it?
- Do you have enough historical examples for training or RAG?

### 4. What's the cost of a mistake?

AI makes errors. The question is whether those errors are recoverable.

A classification system that sometimes misfires on support tickets is low-risk — a human checks and corrects it. An AI system that automatically approves loan applications based on flawed reasoning is high-risk — by the time you catch the error, real damage is done.

Design your AI workflows around the recovery cost of failure, not the optimistic case.

## The Highest-ROI Starting Points (by business type)

**Startups and SMBs:**
- AI-assisted customer support triage (low risk, high volume)
- Document extraction from invoices/contracts
- Lead qualification scoring from CRM data

**Mid-size organizations:**
- Internal knowledge bases (RAG over company documents)
- Report automation from operational dashboards
- Onboarding and HR document automation

**Enterprise:**
- Workflow orchestration across existing systems
- AI audit of existing technology stack for automation opportunities
- Predictive analytics on operational data
